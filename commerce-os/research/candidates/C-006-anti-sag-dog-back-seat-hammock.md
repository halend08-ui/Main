## Customer
Owners of medium/large dogs who drive with the dog on the back seat regularly (park, trailhead, beach, vet) and already own, or have owned, a basic hammock cover that sags, slides or leaks. [INFERENCE from S-B10, S-B11, S-B12]

## Problem
- Dirt, hair, water and claw damage on rear seats — the core job of the category. [INFERENCE from product positioning, S-B13, S-B14]
- Recurring complaints summarised from reviews/tests: dogs slipping on slick surfaces (nails sliding, older dogs sliding on turns), seams tearing, clips breaking (one report after 3 months), sag in the middle letting paws reach the console, seams not waterproof (vomit leaked through), backing disintegrating after washing. [FACT that the summary reports these, S-B10]
- Footwell sag: an oversized/slack cover sags into the footwell so the "floor" is no longer flat; forum users discuss "hard bottom" covers to fix it. [FACT per search summary, S-B11, S-B12]
- Frequency: UNKNOWN.

## Product
Hammock-style rear-seat cover with: (a) textured/rubberised non-slip **top** surface (dog-side), not only non-slip backing; (b) 2–3 removable, foldable stiffener panels that slot into sleeves over the footwell to stop sag while keeping the cover machine-washable once removed; (c) taped/welded seams for real waterproofing; (d) metal (not plastic) headrest buckles; (e) 2 sizes with a fit guide by vehicle class.
Good vs bad: stitch quality, seam sealing, buckle material and top-surface grip decide reviews (S-B10).

## Shipping concerns
Bulky; stiffener panels increase dimensional weight. Non-hazmat. Folding panels needed to keep the box small. [INFERENCE]

## Return / breakage concerns
Vehicle-fit returns (too big for small cars → sag; too small for trucks) [INFERENCE from S-B11]. Plastic panels could crack if bent. Washing durability complaints are common in category (S-B10).

## Creative angles (genuinely different)
1. "Paw-in-the-console" test: dog stands on hammock at a stop — ours stays flat, a generic one sags.
2. Grip test: dog on slick vs textured top surface during a turn (no safety claims).
3. Muddy-trailhead / beach day aftermath: hose off and shake out.
4. Vomit/water pour test on seams (only if verified on samples).
5. Truck crew-cab owners (forum communities discuss this explicitly, S-B12).
6. Senior/arthritic dog comfort angle — careful: comfort, not medical claims.

## Differentiation opportunity
Combine removable stiffeners (hard-bottom benefit) with a washable soft cover and a grippy top surface at mid-price. [INFERENCE] Risk: incumbents already sell hard-bottom variants; differentiation is incremental.

## Risks (regulatory, IP, quality, platform policy)
- Regulatory/claims: US has no crash-test standard for pet travel products; seat covers provide no crash protection. We must **not** make safety/crash claims or imply restraint. [FACT, S-B17]
- IP: hammock covers are generic; specific hard-bottom mechanisms may be patented — UNKNOWN, no patent search done.
- Quality: seams, waterproof membrane, buckles (S-B10).
- Platform: none identified, provided no safety claims.

## Unknowns (what would change the verdict)
- Real landed cost of a stiffener variant and its shipped weight/dimensions.
- Carrier rates for a 3–9 lb bulky parcel.
- Return rate due to fit (UNKNOWN).
- Whether a cheap hard-bottom extender at ~$35–60 already wins on the same promise (S-B16 suggests yes).
