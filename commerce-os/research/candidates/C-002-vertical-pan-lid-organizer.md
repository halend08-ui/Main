## Customer
- [INFERENCE] Home cooks in small kitchens with few lower cabinets who own 5+ pans and lids, especially nonstick/ceramic pans they want to protect. Renters overlap (cannot install pull-outs; ~35% of US households rent [S-A32]).

## Problem
- [FACT] Organizing content describes lids that "never seem to stay put" and pulling one pan out turning "into an avalanche"; some organizers are flimsy and fall over; wire shelves let small pans fall through [S-A17].
- [FACT] Stacking scratches nonstick: sources advise keeping pans from rubbing and say stacking/unstacking damages coatings [S-A17].
- [FACT] Bending down to wrestle stacked heavy cookware is described as hard on knees and back [S-A17].
- [FACT] Some pull-out organizers' retention bars are not tight enough to stop thin lids rolling out when a drawer is opened aggressively [S-A17].
- Workarounds: stacking with felt pan protectors, lid racks on doors, dividers in deep drawers [S-A17].

## Product
An expandable base (≈12–22 in wide) with **tall, adjustable steel dividers** that holds pans, lids, cutting boards and sheet pans upright; heavy-gauge so it supports cast iron; non-slip feet.
- Variant B: same rack on a **no-drill adhesive pull-out slide** (renter-friendly) — adhesive pull-out frames already exist on Amazon [S-A24].
- Good version: thick steel, dividers that lock (don't pop out), heights tall enough for 12 in skillets, rubberized contact points.
- Bad version: thin wire that bends under cast iron, dividers that slide when a pan is removed, short dividers that let lids tip.

## Shipping concerns
- [ASSUMPTION] Flat-pack steel, 2–4 lb, non-fragile, no hazmat. Edges can puncture cartons — needs corner protection.

## Return / breakage concerns
- [INFERENCE] Fit risk is moderate: expandable width mitigates, but cabinet depth/height and face-frame openings can still block it. Divider wobble is a likely "not as described" return reason [S-A17].
- Breakage: low; bent dividers in transit possible.

## Creative angles (genuinely different)
1. "Pan avalanche" vs one-hand grab (demonstration).
2. Nonstick protection: close-up of scratches from stacking vs upright storage [S-A17].
3. Cast-iron strength test (load the rack with a 12 in skillet).
4. Ergonomics: no more kneeling and unstacking [S-A17].
5. Renter pull-out (Variant B): no-drill slide install in 2 minutes [S-A24].
6. Multi-use: sheet pans, cutting boards, lids in one rack.

## Differentiation opportunity
- [INFERENCE] Heavy-gauge dividers that lock + rubber-sleeved contact points + bundled felt pan protectors, positioned as "protects your nonstick". Distinct from $13 thin-wire racks; must avoid copying YouCopia's design (see IP).
