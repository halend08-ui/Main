## Customer
Cat owners (often renters or owners of a newer/expensive sofa) whose cat repeatedly scratches the same spot — usually the sofa arm or front corner — and who have already tried something (tape, spray) that failed. [INFERENCE from the complaint pattern in S-B01, S-B03, S-B06]

## Problem
- Cats scratching upholstered furniture; the damage concentrates on arms/corners. [INFERENCE — product forms on sale are specifically "right arm", "corner" protectors, S-B04, S-B07]
- Scratching is **normal** cat behaviour (marking, claw conditioning, stretching); AAFP guidance is to provide a sturdy scratcher near favoured areas and redirect/reward, not punish. [FACT, S-B09]
- Current workaround = double/single-sided sticky sheets or tape. A search summary of Walmart reviews for one such product reports roughly half of reviewers complaining about poor adhesion (falls off, bunches, sticks to itself during application, fails on some fabrics, leaves residue). [FACT that the summary says this, S-B01; the "roughly half" figure is the search engine's characterisation, not a count I made]
- Forum thread title "Sticky paws to protect furniture? Cat rips it off!" [FACT — title only, page not opened, S-B03]
- Listing copy for clear protectors states they do not work on microfiber, faux or real leather. [FACT per search summary, S-B06]
- Frequency: UNKNOWN (no prevalence statistic found).

## Product
A kit sold per "problem spot" (e.g. 2 sofa arms or 2 corners):
1. Clear, smooth-faced anti-scratch guard panels (deter) with twist pins **and** adhesive options, so it works on fabrics where adhesive fails.
2. A sisal armrest/corner scratching panel that sits immediately beside/over the guarded spot (redirect) — follows the AAFP "put the scratcher where the cat already scratches" logic (S-B09).
3. Fabric-type compatibility card + residue-free removal instructions.
Good version: thick PET/TPU panels that stay flat, pins that don't snag, sisal panel with a non-slip backing that does not need adhesive on the sofa. Bad version: thin film that curls, adhesive that leaves residue, sisal that sheds.

## Shipping concerns
Flat, light, no batteries/liquids/aerosols. Sisal adds mild bulk. Adhesive should be packed with liners to avoid sticking in transit. [INFERENCE]

## Return / breakage concerns
Breakage negligible. Return drivers: adhesion failure on certain fabrics (S-B01), incompatibility with leather/microfiber (S-B06), and cat not changing behaviour (outcome not fully in our control). Mitigation: pins-first design, fabric compatibility chart pre-purchase. [INFERENCE]

## Creative angles (genuinely different)
1. "Don't just block — redirect": vet-guideline framing (cite AAFP guidance accurately, no medical claims) [S-B09].
2. Renter / security-deposit angle: protect the landlord's or your new sofa, residue-free removal.
3. Failure-of-tape angle: "tried sticky tape and your cat peeled it off?" (mirrors S-B03 complaint).
4. Day-1 vs day-14 home footage of the cat choosing the sisal spot.
5. Interior-design angle: clear guard invisible vs ugly throws/blankets over the sofa.
6. Multi-cat household / kitten-proofing a new sofa.

## Differentiation opportunity
System-per-spot (deter + redirect), pin-first attachment for fabrics where adhesive fails, fabric compatibility guidance, matched colours of sisal panel. [INFERENCE] Easily copied by incumbents; moat is brand/content, not product.
