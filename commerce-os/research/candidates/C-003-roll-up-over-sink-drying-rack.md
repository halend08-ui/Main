## Customer
- [INFERENCE] Studio/apartment dwellers and anyone with a small kitchen and no dishwasher (or overflow from one), for whom a countertop dish rack takes most of the usable prep space.

## Problem
- [FACT] Over-the-sink racks are presented as solving the lack of counter space in small kitchens; a studio-apartment resident's account of clearing counter space is cited, and Reddit users reportedly recommend alternatives such as silicone drying mats [S-A11].
- [FACT] Recurring complaints about roll-up racks: rust (incl. steel hidden inside silicone that can rust unseen), instability/bending under heavy cookware, silicone wear and mold if left wet, slipping on sink edges without grips [S-A14].
- [FACT] Review/Q&A summaries report roll-up racks rusting within months to a year; budget racks often use 430 stainless with low corrosion resistance [S-A13].
- Workarounds: silicone drying mats, towels, countertop racks [S-A11].

## Product
Roll-up rack (stainless rods fully over-molded or bare 304/316 stainless) spanning the sink, in 3–4 lengths, with silicone grip ends.
- Bundle concept to reach a viable price: rack + matching silicone drying mat + sink-caddy (ASSUMPTION).
- Good version: verified 304 (or 316) stainless, thick rods (low sag), grippy ends, multiple widths labelled by sink opening.
- Bad version: 430 or plated steel, thin rods that bow, silicone that traps water [S-A13][S-A14].
