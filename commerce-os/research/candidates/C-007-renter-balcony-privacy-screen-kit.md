## Customer
Apartment renters (often mid/high floors) whose balcony faces neighbours or a street, who want privacy without drilling and without violating their lease. [INFERENCE from S-B18, S-B21]

## Problem
- Lack of privacy on balconies; renters cannot drill. Zip-tie/bungee/tension methods are presented as damage-free. [FACT per search summary of blog content, S-B18 — weak source]
- Wind on high floors: light screens can rip off, flap loudly, strain ties or detach; cheap zip ties loosen; included ties are "short and weak" and can break. [FACT per search summaries, S-B18, S-B22 — weak sources]
- Fading: faux-ivy panels with sprayed-on UV inhibitor fade in ~12–18 months in direct sun (per a seller of faux ivy — a competitor's claim). [FACT that the source says so, S-B23]
- Lease/HOA restrictions: some communities forbid screens that block balconies; a renter asked a lawyer forum whether a screen could be a lease violation. [FACT — titles/summaries, S-B21]
- Frequency: UNKNOWN.

## Product
3 ft-high HDPE privacy mesh (≥160–210 gsm, ~90% visibility blockage claims exist in market) in 2–3 lengths, sold as a kit with: UV-stabilised heavy-duty ties **and** reusable bungee balls/clips (no-tie option), reinforced grommets every ~12 in, a railing-measuring guide, and a printable "removable, non-penetrating" description renters can show management. Good vs bad: gsm, grommet reinforcement, UV stabiliser in the yarn, fastener strength.
