## Customer
Knitters and crocheters who work on projects away from a fixed chair — commuting, travelling, waiting rooms, sofa with pets/kids — and carry one work-in-progress (WIP) plus its yarn and notions. [INFERENCE from use-cases described in S-C10, S-C17]

## Problem
- Yarn balls roll away, get dirty and tangle when worked from loose in a bag; tangling worsens when the skein rolls or flips. [FACT per snippets of S-C17]
- Common workaround: poke a hole in a Ziploc or container and feed the yarn through it; wind centre-pull cakes. [FACT per S-C17]
- **Grommets on project bags are polarising:** once yarn is fed through a closed grommet you cannot remove the project without cutting the yarn; with yarn in the grommet the project is hard to put back and zip up; the guide claims most crafters don't use or like grommet bags. [FACT as stated in snippet of S-C10 — single blogger's opinion, not a survey]
- Snagging of yarn on zippers/hook-and-loop is a known buying criterion ("no-snag designs"). [FACT per S-C10 snippet]
- Forum threads titled "Ideal knitting crochet project bag" and "Yarn bags" exist on KnittingParadise [S-C37] — content NOT read (blocked).
- Frequency: every on-the-go session for affected crafters [ASSUMPTION].

## Product
Structured, stand-up zip project bag (~medium WIP size) with:
- 2–3 **open-slot / snap-close yarn guides** so yarn can be fed and removed without cutting (solves the grommet trap);
- snag-free zipper garage/cover and no hook-and-loop inside;
- wipe-clean liner, clear notions pocket, wrist strap / loop to hang on a chair or wrist.
Good vs bad: guides that don't open on their own, no snag points, bag stands open, zipper quality. Bad: closed grommets, rough edges, thin fabric.

## Shipping concerns
Minimal: soft goods, light, poly mailer, no batteries/liquids. Structured bags may need a box to avoid crushing [INFERENCE].

## Return / breakage concerns
Low breakage. Returns mostly aesthetic (colour/print) or size mismatch with project [INFERENCE]. Zipper failure is the main quality-return driver [ASSUMPTION].

## Creative angles (genuinely different)
1. "Never cut your yarn again" — demo opening the guide and lifting the WIP out.
2. Commuter: knitting on a train with the bag closed on the lap.
3. Cat/dog/toddler-proof yarn demo.
4. Multi-colour work: 2–3 guides feeding colourwork strands without twisting.
5. Gift angle for the knitter in your life (holiday).

## Differentiation opportunity
Function-first spec (open guides + snag-free zipper + stand-up structure) at mass-market-ish price, with transparent comparison vs closed-grommet bags. [INFERENCE] Moderate — Della Q "Oh Snap" and snap/grommet hybrids already exist (S-C13, S-C15), so differentiation must come from execution/price/design, not the concept.
